# Tower Wizard demo: pacing report

Generated by `node tools/pacing/sim.js` (headless; the economy is copied from `assets/scripts/TowerWizardGame.ts` as of 2026-10-25).
Siege constants live in `tools/pacing/siege_tuning.json`. `node tools/pacing/sim.js --solve` re-fits the wall and boss HPs. `node tools/pacing/solve_grid.js` searches the upgrade curves. `--log <profile>` prints every purchase.

## What is modelled

**Economy**, exactly as in `TowerWizardGame.ts`:
- **Orb clicks:** 1 × (1 + Wizard Magic) magic, with a 0.25 s cooldown.
- **Click speed boost:** each click speeds things up by +35% plus a per-level bonus for 0.4 s.
- **Cloudlings:** each throws one ball every 4 s. A ball gives 20 × (1 + 0.7·Boost) × (1 + magic totems).
- **Spirit Tomes:** 5 × (1 + 0.5·Text Enchantment) × (1 + knowledge totems) every 10 s.
- **Druids:** one log each every 4 s. A log gives 4 × (1 + 0.5·Bigger Forest) × (1 + forest totems).
- **Totems:** crafted for 150·2^n wood, then carved.
- **Other skills and costs:** Library Spirits, the spirit cost curve, and every unlock.
- **Tutorial grants:** +1 spirit after tower 2 and after tower 3.
- **Towers:** cost is checked against lifetime earned resources, and nothing is deducted.
- **Builds:** the animation takes 4.7 s. Clicks, the study and the forest all stop during it; cloudlings keep producing.
- **Tutorial popups:** each costs the player some reading time.

**Fervor** is not wired into click magic in the game yet; only the meter exists. The sim applies it as click magic × (1 + f), where f is the average fill of the meter, so f = 1 gives FERVOR_MAX_MULT = 2.

**Player bot:** a greedy bot.
- For each resource pool, it buys the item that saves the most estimated seconds on the way to the next tower, per unit of cost.
- Knowledge and spirits are valued through what they can buy.
- It builds each tower as soon as it can.
- It has a reaction delay between shop visits and loses some swiping time per visit.

| profile | swiping | fervor f | shop check | lost per visit | reading per popup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| casual | taps 2/s, 15 s on / 15 s off (50%) | 0 | every 8 s | 1.5 s | 4 s |
| engaged | swipes 4/s continuously | 0.4 | every 3 s | 0.6 s | 2.5 s |
| tryhard | swipes 4/s continuously | 1.0 (full) | every 1.5 s | 0.4 s | 1 s |

**Observations**
- **Engaged is on target.** It reaches tower 4 at 8:02, inside the 8–10 min window. No engaged gap is longer than about 21 s.
- **Wood gates tower 4, not magic.** All profiles finish 350K magic slightly before 20K wood. The bot puts 6 of its 7 totems into Forest totems, because their +4 spirits also feed cloudlings.
- **Tryhard is only about 6% faster in the early game.** Fervor only doubles click magic (at most 40/s). Cloudlings make thousands per second by tower 3. This is acceptable, because the siege is where fervor should pay off (see below).
- **Casual sits for 80 s at tower 1→2.** With about 1 click/s at 3 magic per click, saving 200 magic for the first spirit is slow. This only hurts casual players, and they are still tapping during it.

## Recommended early-game changes

**No change is required** to reach the 8–10 min engaged target or the ≤ ~20 s dead-wait rule. Optional tweaks, in priority order:
1. **Wire fervor into orb-click magic** in the game, as `× (1 + fervor)`. These numbers already assume it. Without it, engaged and tryhard reach tower 4 about 30 s later (8:35 and 8:02 instead of 8:02 and 7:33). Most of that comes from the first two segments.
2. **Optional, casual only:** let tower 1's tutorial unlock Summon Cloudling directly. Today it needs the first `summon_spirit` purchase. Pair this with a granted spirit, as towers 2 and 3 already do. This removes the 80 s casual stall. The current grant at tower 1 is not enough on its own, because the cloudling stays locked until a spirit is bought.
3. **If tower 3→4 feels long** (5 min engaged): lower the wood requirement from 20K to 15K. This saves about 20–40 s (engaged 7:44, casual 11:25, tryhard 7:09). It is not needed for the target.

## Siege model (after tower 4)

The siege has 3 walls (Cloud, Solar, Cosmic) and then the Warden's 3 phases, fought in order.

**Damage sources**
- **Auto bolts:** the orb fires `boltDamage × (fireRate + 0.06 · RapidFire) × power` damage per second.
- **Swipe-bolts:** every orb click, swipe or tap, also fires a swipe-bolt of `swipeDamage × fervorMult × power`.

**Formulas**
- `power = 1.06^BoltPower × tower5 × tower6`.
- `fervorMult = 1 + fervorK · f^fervorExp`, which is **1 + 4·f²**. This gives ×1.64 at f = 0.4 and ×5 at full (gold) fervor.

**Upgrades and towers**
- **Bolt Power** costs 4000·1.15^L magic. **Rapid Fire** costs 6000·1.18^L magic.
- **Towers 5 and 6** each multiply all damage ×2.
  - Tower 5 unlocks after the Cloud Wall and costs 820K lifetime magic.
  - Tower 6 unlocks after the Solar Wall and costs 1.3M lifetime magic.
  - Each build takes 4.7 s.

**Breaks:** there is a 3 s transition with no damage after each wall or phase.

**Economy during the siege:** the economy keeps running. Spirits, knowledge, wood and totems keep being reinvested; magic goes to the siege upgrades.

### Why a non-linear fervor curve is needed

The two constraints pull in opposite directions:
- **Tryhard ≥ 30% faster** needs swipe damage to be most of the engaged player's damage.
- **Casual < 15 min** needs auto damage to dominate, because casual swipes only 1/s versus 4/s.

With linear fervor (1 + K·f), no damage split satisfies both. The grid search found tryhard at ×1.25 with casual at 16:06, or tryhard at ×1.11 with casual at 10:18. Squaring the fill (1 + 4·f²) fixes this: full fervor becomes worth ×5 while an engaged player's f = 0.4 stays at ×1.64. It reads naturally in game, since the gold tier of the existing mint → pink → gold meter is the jackpot.

### SIEGE_TUNING

```json
{
  "walls": [
    { "name": "Cloud Wall", "hp": 25000 },
    { "name": "Solar Wall", "hp": 77000 },
    { "name": "Cosmic Wall", "hp": 200000 }
  ],
  "boss": { "name": "The Warden", "phases": [110000, 120000, 120000] },
  "boltDamage": 30,
  "fireRate": 1,
  "swipeDamage": 10,
  "fervorK": 4,
  "fervorExp": 2,
  "upgrades": {
    "power": { "name": "Bolt Power", "cost0": 4000, "growth": 1.15, "effect": 0.06 },
    "rate":  { "name": "Rapid Fire", "cost0": 6000, "growth": 1.18, "effect": 0.06 }
  },
  "towers": [
    { "level": 5, "after": 1, "cost": { "magic": 820000 }, "damageMult": 2 },
    { "level": 6, "after": 2, "cost": { "magic": 1300000 }, "damageMult": 2 }
  ],
  "transition": 3,
  "buildTime": 4.7
}
```

**Field meanings**
- **Damage:** `boltDamage` is damage per auto bolt; `fireRate` is auto bolts per second; `swipeDamage` is damage per swipe-bolt, one per orb click.
- **Bolt Power:** each level multiplies all damage by 1 + `effect`, so the effect compounds.
- **Rapid Fire:** each level adds `effect` bolts per second.
- **Towers:** `after` is the number of walls that must be broken before the tower can be built. `cost` is checked against lifetime earned magic, like towers 1–4.

When Siege.ts lands, re-check with `node tools/pacing/sim.js --siege <json>` after mirroring its constants into a JSON file of this shape. If the implemented mechanics differ, update `runSiege()`; examples are bolt travel time, damage that depends on magic per click, and a different fervor curve.
